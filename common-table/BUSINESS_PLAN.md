# Common Table — Business Plan

**Partnerships, made warmly.** A Singapore partnership studio that connects homegrown brands, F&B and lifestyle venues, and startups, then helps them build collaborations that work.

> "Common Table" is a working name. Check ACRA name availability and the `.sg` / `.com` domains before committing to it.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Singapore runs on collaborations: UNIQLO x Ya Kun, Old Chang Kee x Talking Toes, Yo-Chi x Tiap Tiap, The Coconut Club x Wacky Wears, Narrative Coffee Stand and Pelle & Pepe sharing a shopfront in Joo Chiat. Big brands have agencies to set these up. Most homegrown brands, cafés and early-stage startups don't. They know a collab would help, but nobody has the time to find the right partner, pitch them, agree terms and run the launch. Common Table does exactly that. You're the person who already loves bringing people together; this turns that into a paid, repeatable service.

## 2. Why you, why now

- **Your edge is relationships, not ads.** Influencer agencies sell reach. You sell *fit*: the right two businesses at the same table. That's harder to copy and builds over time; every match grows your network.
- **Collabs are now a normal marketing line item in SG.** National Day, Lunar New Year, Mid-Autumn and Christmas each bring a wave of limited-edition collabs. Brands plan these 2–4 months ahead, so there's a predictable sales calendar.
- **Low startup cost.** Laptop, phone, website, a contract template. No inventory, no office.

## 3. Who you serve (three lanes)

| Lane | Who | What they want | Typical deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Brand x Brand** | Homegrown fashion, beauty, lifestyle and food brands (10–80 staff) | A co-branded drop, gift set or capsule that reaches the partner's audience | Collab Sprint |
| **B. F&B and lifestyle venues** | Independent cafés, restaurants, bars, gyms, studios | Pop-ups, menu collabs, events that bring new foot traffic | Collab Sprint or Retainer |
| **C. Startups and B2B** | Seed to Series A startups, SMEs entering SG | Warm intros to distributors, channel partners, corporate pilots, suppliers | Intro Package + success fee |

**Start with Lanes A and B together.** They feed each other: a brand needs a venue for its pop-up, and a café needs a brand for its next menu collab. Lane C pays more per deal but takes longer to close, so grow it from month 3.

### Your first 20 conversations
Make a simple list (Notion or a Google Sheet) with these columns: *business, contact, lane, last collab, idea for them, status*. Aim for **5 outreach messages a day**. Never pitch your service first; pitch **a specific collab idea** ("I think your socks and X's kaya toast would make a great National Day drop"). The idea is what gets the reply.

## 5. Services and pricing (SGD)

Benchmarks: boutique marketing agencies in Singapore charge about **S$3,000–8,000/month** on retainer, and finder's fees for business introductions are commonly **5–15%** of deal value (lower for large deals). You price below agencies at first because you're new and focused only on partnerships, then raise prices once you have 3–5 case studies.

| Service | What's included | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Coffee Chat** | 30-min call to understand the business and spot 2–3 collab ideas | **Free** (your lead magnet) |
| **The Warm Intro** | 3 vetted partner matches, a one-page brief on each, and a personal introduction | **S$800** per package |
| **Collab Sprint** | One full collaboration from idea to launch: concept, partner sourcing, terms, timeline, launch-day coordination (4–8 weeks) | **S$3,500–6,000** depending on scale |
| **Partnerships Retainer** | Ongoing partner pipeline: 2–4 qualified intros a month, one collab managed per quarter, monthly report | **S$2,500/month** (3-month minimum) |
| **B2B Intro (success-based)** | Intros to distributors, channel partners or corporate buyers | **S$500 upfront + 8% of first-year contract value**, capped at S$15,000 |

**Pricing rules**
- **Always take a deposit:** 50% upfront on Sprints, retainers billed monthly in advance.
- **Success fees need a signed agreement** before you make the intro, or they're hard to enforce. Use a simple finder's fee agreement covering percentage, timing, and a 12-month window.
- **Launch offer:** first 3 clients get 30% off the Collab Sprint in exchange for a testimonial and permission to use the collab as a case study.
- **Raise prices** by ~20% after every 3 completed projects until demand slows.

## 6. Community engine: "Common Table Suppers"

This is your own events series, and it plays to what you enjoy most.
- Once a month, 12–20 founders and brand owners share a long-table dinner at a partner venue.
- **Tickets S$55–80.** The venue gets a full house on a quiet weeknight (a Lane B pitch in itself), and a brand sponsors a gift bag (a Lane A pitch).
- **Why it matters:** it's your best source of leads, your best source of collab ideas, and it makes your brand feel like the warm, welcoming business you want it to be.

## 7. How a deal works

1. **Coffee Chat**: learn their goals, audience, budget and timeline.
2. **Match**: shortlist partners from your network and research.
3. **Warm intro**: a personal message or a three-way coffee, never a cold email.
4. **Shape the collab**: who does what, who pays for what, how revenue is split, what success looks like.
5. **Paper it**: simple one-page MOU between the partners plus your service agreement.
6. **Launch**: timeline, content, on-the-day coordination.
7. **Wrap-up**: results, photos, a testimonial, and "what's next?" (this is how Sprints turn into retainers).

| Revenue line | Assumption | Year 1 |

| **Total revenue** | | **~S$91,000** |

| Cost | Year 1 |
|---|---|
| ACRA registration, domain, website hosting, email | ~S$400 |

This is very lean: almost all revenue is profit, and your time is the real constraint. Once you're handling more than about 4 active projects, consider a part-time assistant or a junior partner on commission.

## 9. 90-day launch plan

**Days 1–30: Set up**
- Register as a sole proprietorship with ACRA (via BizFile+), open a business bank account.
- Publish the website (`website/index.html` in this folder) and set up a Calendly link for Coffee Chats.
- Write your service agreement and finder's fee template.
- Build your prospect list of 50 (start with section 4).
- Post on LinkedIn and Instagram that you're launching, with your first collab idea as the hook.

**Days 31–60: First clients**
- 5 outreach messages a day, 3–5 Coffee Chats a week.
- Close the first 2 Collab Sprints at the launch discount.
- Host your first Common Table Supper.
- Aim one pitch at the **Lunar New Year 2027** season (brands plan from Nov–Dec).

**Days 61–90: Proof and repeat**
- Deliver and document the first collabs as case studies on the website.
- Convert at least 1 Sprint client to a retainer.
- Start Lane C: attend 2 startup events a month and offer B2B intros.

## 10. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Clients go around you after the intro | Signed agreement **before** sharing names; success-fee window of 12 months |
| A collab flops and hurts your reputation | Only match businesses whose audiences and values genuinely fit; be willing to say no |
| Lumpy income | Push retainers; use the festive calendar to book work 3 months ahead |
| Burnout from too many coffees | Batch Coffee Chats on 2 days a week; let Suppers do group networking |
| Looks like "just another agency" | Lead with specific collab ideas and the warm, community-first brand |

## 11. Key metrics to track monthly
- New Coffee Chats booked
- Chat → paid client conversion rate (aim for 25–35%)
- Active retainers
- Collabs launched
- Revenue and average deal size
- Supper attendance and referrals from Suppers
