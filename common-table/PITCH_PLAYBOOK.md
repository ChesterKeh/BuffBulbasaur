# Common Table — How the business works & how to pitch

## 1. The business in one picture

```
   Business A  ──┐                         ┌──  Business B
 (e.g. a café)   │   YOU: find the match,  │  (e.g. a skincare label)
                 ├─► shape the idea, make  ◄┤
                 │   the intro, run the    │
                 │   launch                │
                 └────────► COLLAB ◄────────┘
                   (pop-up, co-branded drop,
                    menu, event, deal)
```

You sit in the middle. Both businesses want new customers, buzz or a deal, but neither has the time, contacts or confidence to find a partner and make it happen. You do that part, and you get paid for it.

### Where the money comes from
| Product | Who pays | Price | When you get paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coffee chat | Nobody | Free | Your lead magnet |
| Warm Intro | The business that asked | S$800 | Upfront |
| Collab Sprint | Usually the business that started it (sometimes both split it) | S$3.5k–6k | 50% to start, 50% on launch |
| Retainer | One business, ongoing | S$2,500/month | Monthly in advance |
| B2B Intro | The startup | S$500 + 8% of first-year deal (capped) | S$500 upfront, % when the deal closes |
| Suppers | Guests (and later a sponsor) | ~S$60/seat | Ticket sales |

### A worked example
1. A Joo Chiat café has quiet weekdays. You offer a free coffee chat.
2. In the chat you suggest: "A skincare brunch with a homegrown skincare label. Their 20k followers come to you."
3. They like it. They pay a **Collab Sprint** (say S$3,500, 50% upfront).
4. You approach 2–3 skincare labels with the idea. One says yes; they get a free, ready-made activation (you can charge them too, but often you don't on the first ones).
5. You agree who provides what, the revenue split, dates and content. Both sign a one-page agreement.
6. Launch weekend. You coordinate, take photos and collect results.
7. That becomes your first real case study on the website, and you offer both sides the next collab or a retainer.

## 2. Who to pitch (in this order)

### Start here: cafés and venues (easiest yes)
- **Why first:** they feel the pain every day (quiet weekdays) and the owner is usually on site. You can walk in.
- **Who to talk to:** the owner or manager. Never the counter staff.
- **Best time:** weekdays 2–5pm (between lunch and dinner).
- **Where:** independent cafés in Joo Chiat, Tiong Bahru, Jalan Besar, Keong Saik and Tanjong Pagar; cafés that already do collabs (Prairie by Craftsmen, Brawn & Brains, The Coconut Club, Narrative Coffee Stand).

### Next: homegrown brands with a collab track record
- **Why:** they already know collabs work, so you skip the education.
- **Who to talk to:** the founder (small brands) or the marketing / brand manager (bigger brands). Find them on LinkedIn or by DMing the brand on Instagram.
- **Names from your business plan:** Tiap Tiap, Talking Toes, Wacky Wears, Love Bonito, Sigi Skin, Allies of Skin, SADSHRIMPS (illustrator), and the brands in DORS HOMEGROWN and STB "Made With Passion".
- **Timing:** brands plan festive collabs 2–4 months ahead. **Right now (late Nov) is the time to pitch Lunar New Year 2027 and Valentine's.**

### Later (month 3+): startups and B2B
- Slower to close but bigger fees. Meet them at Startup Grind Singapore, Founder Institute and Singapore Entrepreneurs Network events.

## 3. The golden rule: pitch an idea, not a service
Nobody wants to buy "partnership services". Everybody wants to hear "I think your socks and X's kaya toast would sell out on National Day."
- Before every pitch, write **one specific collab idea** for that business.
- The service and the price come up only after they say "ooh, how would that work?"

### The two-sided trick (how to start with zero clients)
1. Get a soft yes from Business A: "If I found a skincare label up for this, would you be interested?"
2. Go to Business B: "A café in Joo Chiat wants to do a skincare brunch with a homegrown label. Would you be up for it?"
3. Now both sides are interested because the other one is. That's your first deal.

## 4. Get ready before you pitch (checklist)
- [ ] Business name confirmed; ACRA registration (sole proprietorship via BizFile+)
- [ ] Instagram account with 6–9 posts (collab ideas, Singapore collabs you love, your why)
- [ ] Website live, with your real email in `content.js`
- [ ] **Collab menu**: a one-page PDF with what you do, 3 example ideas, prices and how to book a chat
- [ ] **Idea bank**: 20 collab ideas (pairs of business types + the idea)
- [ ] **Prospect tracker** (Google Sheet): business · contact · type · their last collab · my idea for them · status · next step
- [ ] Calendar link for coffee chats (Calendly or Google Calendar booking page)
- [ ] **Two templates**: (1) your service agreement and (2) a one-page collab agreement between partners. A simple finder's-fee clause must be signed **before** you share the other side's name. Get these checked once by a lawyer.
- [ ] PayNow / bank details on your invoice template

## 5. Scripts you can copy

### Instagram DM (to a brand)
> Hi [Name]! Big fan of [specific product/post]. I run Common Table: we match homegrown brands and cafés for collabs. I had an idea for you: **[one-line idea]**. I've got a café in mind whose crowd is very much your crowd. Open to a 20-min coffee? My treat ☕

### Email (to a marketing manager)
> **Subject:** A Lunar New Year collab idea for [Brand]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm [your name], founder of Common Table, a Singapore partnership studio that matches homegrown brands, cafés and venues for collabs.
>
> Seeing your [recent collab/launch], I think [Brand] × [type of partner] could be a great fit for Lunar New Year: **[idea in 1–2 sentences]**.
>
> I'd handle partner sourcing, terms and the launch plan. Would a 20-minute coffee next week work? [calendar link]
>
> Warmly,
> [Name] · [website]

### Walk-in (café owner)
> "Hi, are you the owner? I'm [name], I run Common Table: I help cafés fill quiet days by pairing them with local brands for pop-ups and menu collabs. I had an idea for your space: [idea]. Can I leave my card and buy you a coffee sometime this week?"

### Follow-up (after 4–5 days of no reply)
> Hi [Name], just floating this back up. Still think [idea] would be a lovely fit for you. Happy to share 2–3 other ideas too if this one isn't it 😊

## 6. The 30-minute coffee chat
1. **5 min:** about them: their story, customers, what's working.
2. **10 min:** their goal: more customers, buzz for a launch, a festive moment, a deal?
3. **10 min:** share 2–3 ideas. Watch which one lights them up.
4. **5 min:** next step: "Want me to sound out partners for that one? Here's how it works: [Warm Intro S$800 or Collab Sprint from S$3.5k]. For our first three partners it's 30% off."

**Then:** send a one-page proposal within 24 hours (idea, what you'll do, price, timeline).

### When they say…
- **"How much?"** → "Depends on size. A full collab is S$3.5–6k, or start with a Warm Intro at S$800. Founding partners get 30% off."
- **"We can do it ourselves."** → "Totally! The hard part is finding the right partner and making it happen alongside running your business. That's the bit I take off your plate."
- **"We've never done a collab."** → "Perfect, that's why we start small: one weekend, one partner, low risk."
- **"Send me something."** → Send the collab menu PDF and book a follow-up date then and there.

## 7. Your first two weeks
| Days | Do |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Register the business, set up Instagram, put your real email in the website, publish it |
| 3–4 | Build the prospect tracker (30 names) and write an idea for each |
| 5–10 | 5 messages a day + walk into 2–3 cafés on weekday afternoons |
| 6–14 | Coffee chats; aim for 5 chats and 1–2 "yes, let's try" |
| 14 | Pick a venue and set a date for your first Common Table Supper (invite everyone you've met) |

**Goal for month one:** 1 paying founding client and 1 supper. Document everything, because that becomes the first real collab on your website.
